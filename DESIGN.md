# DESIGN.md — Claw Services company landing page

## Purpose

This document describes the desired look, feel, structure, and product positioning for the main Claw Services public landing page.

This is the company homepage for Claw Services, not the private client portal and not only the service-desk product page. The service desk/request portal is the operating system Claw uses to deliver work, but the homepage should sell the whole company: a premium digital services company that handles a wide range of business, creative, technical, and operational work.

## Company positioning

Claw Services is a human-operated, AI-assisted digital services company.

We help clients turn rough needs into finished digital outcomes: websites, landing pages, lead systems, automations, content, design assets, research, admin support, artist/business operations, and recurring digital execution.

The core promise:

> Bring us the digital work you need done. We scope it, organize it, execute it, and deliver it through a clear operator-led process.

Claw should feel broader than a website agency, more accountable than an AI tool, and more structured than hiring a freelancer.

## What Claw does

Claw Services can include:

- Premium websites and landing pages
- Brand and visual direction
- Lead intake systems and request flows
- Automations and operational workflows
- Content packs, one-pagers, decks, and launch materials
- Research packs, competitor scans, source-backed lists, and admin work
- Artist, creator, and small-business digital operations
- Ongoing operator desk support for recurring work
- Future private client portal for requests, messages, artifacts, credits, approvals, and delivery tracking

The homepage should communicate the whole service range first. The portal/request desk should appear as the method of delivery, not the only thing being sold.

## Audience

Primary audience:

- Small businesses and service companies that need a more professional digital presence
- Founders and operators who need work done without managing many tools or contractors
- Artists, creators, and teams that need repeatable digital execution
- People with messy, unclear requests who need someone to turn the request into a plan and finished deliverables

They may not know whether they need a website, automation, deck, design system, research pack, or content. Claw should make the first step feel simple.

## Desired feeling

The brand should feel:

- Premium but approachable
- Capable and practical
- Human-operated, not robotic
- Calm, clean, and trustworthy
- Operationally sharp
- Creative but not chaotic
- AI-assisted without looking like a generic AI startup
- Broad-service company, not a single SaaS product

Avoid:

- Generic SaaS dashboard landing page
- Cybersecurity/gaming/aggressive claw visuals
- Dark neon AI styling
- A page that only sells the request portal
- A freelancer portfolio vibe
- A plain agency template
- Overly technical developer-tool positioning
- Low-contrast gray text
- Random decorative gradients with no structure

## Visual direction

Use the existing Claw Services visual language as the base:

- Warm off-white / paper background
- Soft ice-blue wash
- Fine grid texture
- Heavy dark navy typography
- Rounded white cards with soft borders
- Subtle shadows and depth
- Mint/teal as calm operational signal color
- Small restrained gold as premium accent
- Occasional dark navy panels for contrast and proof areas
- Premium stock/photo treatment with dark overlays and clear captions

The page should look polished enough for a serious business while still feeling accessible to local businesses, artists, and operators.

## Color system

Use a warm light system with high contrast.

Core tokens:

- Ink / primary text: `#080D1D`
- Deep navy panel: `#101827`
- Warm paper: `#FFFDF8`
- Cream base: `#F7F1E7`
- Ice blue: `#EAF8FF`
- Mint surface: `#E8F7F2`
- Deep teal: `#1F5552`
- Cyan accent: `#73DFE8`
- Premium gold: `#E5BD61`
- Border line: `#D8E1EC`
- Muted text: `#5E6C80` / `#647184`

Accessibility rule: body copy and navigation must remain readable. Do not use faint gray text. Text over images must have strong overlays or solid surfaces behind it.

## Typography

Use a confident modern sans-serif similar to Geist, Inter, or SF Pro.

Headlines:

- Big, bold, tight tracking
- Clear hierarchy
- Editorial confidence
- Simple language

Body:

- Clear and spacious
- Large enough to read comfortably
- Strong line height
- No tiny low-contrast copy

## Homepage structure

Create a full scrolling web homepage. It should feel like the main company site for Claw Services.

Required sections:

### 1. Header / navigation

Navigation should position Claw as a company with multiple capabilities.

Suggested nav:

- Services
- How it works
- Work examples / Capabilities
- Portal
- Start a request

Primary CTA:

- Start a request

Secondary CTA:

- See services

### 2. Hero: company-level positioning

The hero should not only say “request desk.” It should introduce Claw Services as a digital services company.

Possible headline directions:

- Digital services for the work your business keeps putting off.
- One company for websites, systems, content, research, and digital operations.
- Bring the request. Claw turns it into finished digital work.
- Websites, automations, content, research, and operator support — handled end to end.

Hero should include:

- Large confident headline
- Clear subcopy about broad digital services
- Service category chips
- Primary CTA to start a request
- Secondary CTA to explore services
- A visual system/card showing how rough requests become scoped work and delivered assets

### 3. Broad services overview

Show Claw’s service range as the main story.

Service groups:

1. Websites and landing pages
   - Premium homepage, service pages, mobile polish, lead forms, launch support
2. Systems and automations
   - Intake forms, routing, notifications, sheets/CRMs, follow-up flows
3. Content and design
   - Copy, social assets, one-pagers, decks, brand polish, visual packages
4. Research and admin
   - Source-backed research, lead lists, competitor scans, scoring, cleanup work
5. Operator desk
   - Recurring digital work, status tracking, request queue, approvals, delivery archive

Each service card should make the deliverables concrete. Avoid vague labels like “AI solutions.”

### 4. How Claw works

Explain the operating model.

Steps:

1. Send the rough ask
2. Claw clarifies scope, timeline, price, and success criteria
3. Operators execute using AI-assisted workflows where useful
4. Client reviews and approves deliverables
5. Work is saved, tracked, and can continue through the portal/desk

This section should make the service desk feel like the operating layer behind delivery.

### 5. Proof / trust section

Show why Claw is different.

Position against alternatives:

- Not a random freelancer
- Not a black-box AI tool
- Not a bloated agency process
- Not a DIY template stack

Claw is:

- Human accountable
- AI-assisted where it helps
- Delivery-focused
- Clear about scope and next steps
- Built for people who need outcomes, not tools

### 6. Capabilities / work examples

Create a visual section showing practical examples of what Claw can produce.

Example tiles:

- Service business homepage refresh
- Lead intake flow
- Artist release rollout assets
- Research brief and outreach list
- Monthly operations queue
- Client portal artifact delivery

These can be represented as polished UI cards, image tiles, checklist cards, artifact previews, or brief cards.

### 7. The portal / service desk as operating system

The portal should be introduced as the delivery medium, not the whole company.

Message:

Claw uses a request desk/portal to keep work organized: requests, briefs, messages, files, approvals, credits, and delivery history.

Show a tasteful portal preview, but keep it secondary to the main service company positioning.

### 8. Plans / ways to work

Show simple ways to engage:

- One-off request
- Credit package
- Monthly operator desk
- Custom scope

Keep this high-level. Do not over-focus on pricing unless needed.

### 9. Final CTA

End with a direct, simple ask.

CTA copy options:

- Start your first request
- Tell us what you need handled
- Bring us the rough ask

Support copy:

No perfect brief required. Send the outcome you want, the materials you have, and the timeline. Claw will turn it into a plan.

## Component style

Use:

- Large hero typography
- Service category chips
- Rounded cards
- Operator/status chips
- Brief preview cards
- Work artifact cards
- Photo cards with readable overlays
- Dark navy contrast sections
- Soft grid backgrounds
- Subtle line diagrams showing request → scope → delivery
- Clear CTAs

Avoid making the page feel like a software dashboard. It should be a premium company landing page with service-desk UI elements used as supporting proof.

## Imagery direction

Use imagery that feels professional, premium, and service-oriented:

- Clean workspaces
- People collaborating
- Laptops/work reviews
- Strategy walls
- Studio/creative operations
- Client delivery / planning contexts

Avoid cheesy corporate stock photos, over-smiling teams, robot imagery, or literal AI/computer brain visuals.

## Copy tone

Tone should be direct, capable, and simple.

Use language like:

- “Digital work handled end to end.”
- “Bring the rough ask.”
- “We turn it into scope, timeline, price, and deliverables.”
- “AI-assisted intake. Human-operated execution.”
- “Websites, automations, content, research, and ongoing digital support.”

Avoid:

- “Revolutionary AI-powered solutions”
- “Unlock your potential”
- “Seamless synergy”
- Vague agency marketing claims
- Overpromising automation with no human accountability

## Responsive behavior

Desktop:

- Wide editorial hero
- Strong visual cards on the right
- Services as cards or horizontal feature rows
- Portal preview can sit as a product proof panel

Mobile:

- Header simplified
- Hero readable without tiny text
- Service cards stacked
- CTA always easy to find
- No horizontal overflow
- Portal preview simplified into cards rather than a cramped dashboard

## Stitch-specific instructions

Use the existing Claw landing page as source direction, but shift the emphasis from “request desk product” to “Claw Services as a broad digital services company.”

Keep the premium light visual system. Improve the company-level story, service range, and page hierarchy.

Do not produce only a portal/product page. The portal/request desk should appear as the delivery method and client operating layer, not the only service.

Generate a full scrolling web homepage with complete sections, responsive layouts, strong contrast, and native UI components.

If source screenshots or repo files are provided, use them for visual language only. Do not embed screenshots literally inside the design. Recreate the design as native web UI.

## Success criteria

The final page should make a first-time visitor understand in under five seconds:

1. Claw Services handles many types of digital work.
2. The work is delivered through a clear operator-led process.
3. AI helps with intake and execution, but humans are accountable.
4. The service desk/portal organizes delivery, but Claw is broader than the portal.
5. The next step is easy: send the rough request.
